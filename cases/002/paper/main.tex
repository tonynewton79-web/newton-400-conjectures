\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
The 2026 paper \emph{The Cycle Counts of Graphs} conjectures the exact exceptional counts of simple cycles in finite three-connected simple graphs. We start a separate constructive programme with wheel graphs and wheels carrying one added rim chord. An elementary path classification gives an exact two-parameter cycle-count formula, valid for all admissible parameters. An independently implemented exact cycle enumerator verifies 406 graph instances with rim lengths through 30, including the graph-theoretic three-connectivity condition after every two-vertex deletion. This constitutes an exact restricted-family certificate, \emph{not} a solution of the open 2026 conjecture, and no historical originality claim for the elementary construction is made.

\section{The open 2026 cycle-count question}
Write $c(G)$ for the number of unoriented simple cycles in a finite graph. McCulloch, McKay, Salahshoori, and Zaslavsky \cite{CC2026}, \S6(a), conjecture that the positive integers unattainable as $c(G)$ for finite simple 3-connected graphs are precisely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exceptions}
1\text{--}6,\quad8\text{--}12,\quad16\text{--}20,\quad27,\ 30,\ 32\text{--}34.
\end{equation}
They report attainment of every other count through 3000. This is a finite computational range and does not prove eventual coverage. A family of certified constructive cycle counts is therefore a useful, but preliminary, attack on the conjecture.
\section{Wheel and one-chord constructions}
Let $W(m)$ ($m\ge3$) be the wheel with hub $h$ and rim $v_0v_1\cdots v_{m-1}v_0$. For $m\ge4$ and $2\le k\le m-2$, let $W(m;k)$ add the nonexisting rim chord $v_0v_k$.
\begin{theorem}[Exact two-parameter construction]\label{thm:wheel}
Every $W(m)$ and $W(m;k)$ is a three-connected simple graph. Their numbers of simple cycles are
\begin{align}
c(W(m))&=m(m-1)+1,\label{eq:wheel}\\
c(W(m;k))&=\frac{3m^2-5m+8+2k(m-k)}2.\label{eq:chord}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After deleting at most two vertices of $W(m)$, a surviving hub connects every remaining rim vertex; if the hub is deleted, at most one rim vertex is deleted, leaving a connected rim path. Hence the wheel is three-connected. Adding an edge cannot destroy this property.

A wheel cycle is either the rim cycle, or contains the hub and is determined by an unordered pair of distinct rim vertices together with a choice of either of the two rim arcs between them. Thus $c(W(m))=1+2\binom m2$.

Every new cycle formed by adding chord $v_0v_k$ corresponds bijectively to a pre-existing simple $v_0$--$v_k$ path. Put $\ell=m-k$. There are two paths avoiding $h$: the two rim arcs. Paths containing $h$ are split by their two spokes into disjoint rim subpaths. If the first spoke is used immediately at $v_0$, there are $m-1$ choices for the remaining subpath. If only the final spoke is used immediately at $v_k$, there are $m-2$ further choices. When both spokes meet internal rim vertices, the subpaths can lie on opposite rim arcs in $2(k-1)(\ell-1)$ ways, or on the same arc in $\binom{k-1}{2}+\binom{\ell-1}{2}$ ways. These cases are disjoint and exhaustive. Consequently the number of new cycles is
\begin{align*}
\Delta_{m,k}={}&2+(m-1)+(m-2)+2(k-1)(\ell-1)\\
&+\binom{k-1}{2}+\binom{\ell-1}{2}\\
={}&\frac{m^2+2k(m-k)-3m+6}{2}.
\end{align*}
Adding this to $m(m-1)+1$ yields \eqref{eq:chord}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Symmetry and counting scope]
The identity is unchanged by $k\leftrightarrow m-k$, the natural wheel-reflection symmetry. Even the collection of all $W(m;k)$ has a sparse set of cycle counts; we do not infer all-integer coverage from \eqref{eq:chord}. Nor do we claim that the elementary formula has no prior occurrence in the graph-enumeration literature.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact reproducibility and independent replay}
The standalone Python source \texttt{cases/002/verify.py} constructs every graph directly from its adjacency sets. It enumerates simple cycles with the minimum-labelled cycle vertex fixed, then divides the oriented count by two; this prevents rotational and reversal overcounting without importing a graph-library cycle routine. It independently verifies each instance's cycle count against \eqref{eq:wheel} or \eqref{eq:chord}. For connectivity, every pair of vertices is removed and the surviving graph is checked by graph traversal.

For $m=3,\dots,30$, the tests cover 28 wheels and 378 wheels with one admissible chord, yielding \textbf{406 exact cycle-count checks} and \textbf{406 complete two-vertex-deletion connectivity checks}; all pass. The certificate is an exhaustive check over this specified finite test family, not an all-parameter computation. The all-parameter assertion is proved above by combinatorial counting.
\section{The next research and falsification gate}
The constructive search should next add two or three rim chords and quotient placements by the dihedral group, derive their simple-path contribution polynomials, and test whether finitely many parameterized count families jointly cover complete integer intervals. A candidate interval-coverage theorem needs an exact integer proof, independent graph generation, and separate Lean or comparable formalization before being counted as a new conjecture resolution. A counterexample to the 2026 conjecture would instead require an unattainable count not listed in \eqref{eq:exceptions}, with an exhaustive obstruction certificate over \emph{all} 3-connected simple graphs, not merely an unsuccessful search.
